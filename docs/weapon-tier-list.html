How the numbers and tiers are set

Theoretical DPS is the maximum: damage per hit, times pellets, times shots per second. Full damage at any range, every shot on target, no recoil, no reloading.

Actual DPS is that number scaled by what really happens at fighting distances (reloads still not counted; the detail panel gives both with reloads too). I take 5, 10, 20, 35 and 55 m (weighted 15, 25, 30, 20 and 10 percent) and at each one apply three things:

  • Drop-off: the weapon's own falloff curve, and its maximum range where it has one.
  • Spread: each shot lands anywhere in a box of ±spread radians, as the game fires it; the chance it hits a body 1.0 m wide and 1.9 m tall at that distance.
  • Recoil: the game's own recoil profile. Half of the rise you get while holding the trigger is assumed to be pulled back by the player; the rest shifts the aim point up, and the side kick widens the spread.

Tiers are by rank on power, the square root of actual DPS with reloads times actual kill speed (300 HP over the time to kill, reloads and misses included). Top 3 are SS, then 6 S, 8 A, 13 B, 13 C, 6 D and the bottom 3 F. The shape is the point: most weapons should be ordinary.

Not counted: explosive splash, headshots, the price, lobbed shots missing on the move. Weapons that exist to slow or stun fall to the bottom because their damage is the least of what they do. The minigun includes its 2 s spool-up and the Chain Gun is at full spin. The shot check runs once a frame, so at 60 fps the fastest guns (P90, Vector, minigun) fire about a third slower than listed. ◆ marks guns whose DPS at 5 m is at least double their all-range number: a shotgun is judged at the ranges it does not want.
